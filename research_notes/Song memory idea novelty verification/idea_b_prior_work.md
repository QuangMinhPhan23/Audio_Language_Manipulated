# Idea B prior work: do music generators "remember" songs from a title alone? (verification as of 2026-10-22)

## Q0. Overall verdict: has Idea B, or its planner-clamp decomposition, already been done?

### Takeaway
I found no paper, issue or forum thread that prompts open text-to-music models with a song title alone and scores the output against known song attributes. I also found nothing that clamps ACE-Step 1.5's planner metadata to measure what is left over. **But the novelty is narrower than the existing check says.** Its claim that Ghost in the Encoder does "not [study] the planner" is **WRONG**. That paper (ISMIR 2026) runs ACE-Step 1.5 with the chain-of-thought (CoT) planner on and off. With the planner on, outputs are more often matched to the original recording by a cover-song detector (CLEWS). The authors explicitly suggest the planner caption "may function as a song-recognition trigger", and they leave this for future work. A second 2026 paper already measures how well ACE-Step 1.5 obeys key instructions, which is the "renderer obedience" part of Idea B. I would keep the score at 3/5 at most, and raise the risk of being scooped: the Ghost authors have publicly named the next step.

### Cited Findings
- Ghost in the Encoder compares a "no-CoT" setup (planner off) with a "with-CoT" setup (planner on: it "derives a caption and infers BPM/key" from the lyrics) on ACE-Step 1.5, acestep-v15-turbo, with bpm=None and key=None. — [Vohra et al., arXiv 2609.39552 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39552)
- Cover-retrieval results (CLEWS Retrieval@K, N=2,000): with-CoT 0.055 / no-CoT 0.022 / chance 0.005 at K=10; at K=400 they are 0.449 / 0.333 / 0.200 (Table 3). Quote: "the LM caption may function as a song-recognition trigger, surfacing training-data reproductions that lyric conditioning alone does not. We leave systematic investigation of this pathway to future work." — [arXiv 2609.39552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39552)
- From informal listening, Ghost reports that with-CoT songs "bear noticeably more musical and structural resemblance to the original tracks than no-CoT". — [arXiv 2609.39552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39552)
- The model is given lyrics only. The artist's name is removed from the lyrics by phonetic replacement, and **no titles** are used. The data is the top 20 Genius songs of each of 100 Last.fm artists, released before 2024. — [arXiv 2609.39552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39552)
- Ghost is an ISMIR 2026 paper: "in Proc. of the 27th Int. Society for Music Information Retrieval Conf., Abu Dhabi, UAE, 2026." — [arXiv 2609.39552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39552)
- "Beyond Prompted Agreement: Instruction Effects on Key and Beat Grouping in Text-to-Music Generation" (Yining Wang, Bilibili; arXiv 2608.11899, v1 12 Aug 2026, v2 18 Oct 2026). It tests ACE-Step 1.5 (acestep-v15-xl-sft + acestep-5Hz-lm-4B, thinking on, key passed in the native field), Stable Audio 3 and LeVo2 on 2,304 outputs. Exact-key attainment is "66.7% and 67.4%" for ACE-Step and Stable Audio 3, and ACE-Step reaches three-beat grouping 26.6% of the time. Song titles do not appear. — [arXiv 2608.11899 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11899); [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11899)
- Searches of the GitHub issue trackers for ACE-Step-1.5 ("memoriz", "famous song", "song title", "artist name", "copyright"), audiocraft ("song title", "memorization") and stable-audio-tools ("copyright") turned up no thread on title prompting or memorization. The only copyright-related issues were unrelated bugs, plus ACE-Step v1 issue #58, where a maintainer says of the outputs "This belongs to you" and "you may not be able to use this to win a lawsuit". — [ACE-Step issue #58](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step/issues/58)

### Inferences
- What is still unpublished, as far as I found: (i) **title-only** input. Ghost uses lyrics, GEMA used lyrics plus title, and Bob's Confetti uses lyrics. (ii) Reading the planner's YAML fields (BPM, key) and scoring them against the true song's tempo and key or against MGPHot. Ghost never reports the planner's BPM or key values; it only probes caption embeddings. (iii) The clamp intervention: overriding the planner metadata and measuring the remaining title effect. (iv) Wrong-title controls matched on decade and genre.
- What is no longer novel: the claim that "the planner pathway increases resemblance to the original" (Ghost, lyrics-triggered, already shown), and the claim that "ACE-Step only partly obeys explicit key/BPM fields" (2608.11899 already shows about two-thirds exact-key attainment).
- Idea B should cite Ghost as the direct motivation ("Ghost found the CoT pathway raises cover retrieval; we isolate which planner outputs carry it, using titles rather than lyrics"). It should report CLEWS Retrieval@K so the numbers can be compared with Ghost's.
- The 66.7% exact-key attainment sets a ceiling: even a perfect planner key will show up in the audio only about two-thirds of the time. The renderer-obedience arm should use this as its baseline rather than claim to discover it.

### Gaps
- Discord (the ACE-Step server), Reddit and Hugging Face discussion boards could not be searched properly. One Reddit-targeted web search returned nothing relevant. An informal title-prompt experiment posted there cannot be ruled out.
- I did not check the full ISMIR 2026 accepted-paper and late-breaking-demo lists, the Interspeech 2026 proceedings, or NeurIPS/ICML 2026 workshop papers one by one. See Q4.

## Q1. Primary-source check of the papers cited in the existing novelty document

### Takeaway
Most claims hold up. Three need correcting: (a) Ghost in the Encoder does study the planner pathway; (b) "Tracing Inputs, Verifying Outputs" is not a survey, and the audit table sits in its appendix; (c) the Suno/Udio prompt paper was *submitted* to TISMIR, and I could not confirm it was published in May 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Ghost in the Encoder (2609.39552): PARTLY CONFIRMED.** Confirmed: title "Ghost in the Encoder: Decodable Artist Identity Representations in Lyrics-to-Song Generation"; authors Arhan Vohra, Choenden Kyirong, Laura Ibáñez-Martínez and Martín Rocamora; submitted 30 Sep 2026; "a controlled case study of ACE-Step 1.5 spanning 2,000 songs across 100 artists"; artist identity is decodable "from song lyrics alone" and "propagates from the lyric encoder to the diffusion backbone". The lyric-encoder probe peaks at layer 5 with accuracy 0.361 (chance 0.01). **WRONG** in the existing check: "not the planner". RQ5 studies the CoT language model (see Q0). It also states: "Since ACE-Step 1.5's 1.8M-song training corpus is undocumented, training-set membership cannot be verified directly." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39552); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39552)
- **The Name-Free Gap (2509.00654): CONFIRMED.** Nagarajan and Dong, 31 Aug 2025. Uses MusicGen-small and two artists (Billie Eilish, Ludovico Einaudi), with fifteen 15-second reference excerpts per artist. Metrics are VGGish and CLAP embeddings plus a min-distance attribution metric. Quote: "artist names are the strongest control signal across both artists". The paper does not test song titles. — [arXiv 2509.00654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00654)
- **Bob's Confetti (2507.17937): CONFIRMED.** Roh, Novack, Peng, Mireshghallah, Berg-Kirkpatrick and Houmansadr; v1 23 Jul 2025, v4 25 Feb 2026. The attack (Adversarial PhoneTic Prompting, APT) replaces lyrics with words that sound alike. It is tested on Suno and YuE, and also Veo 3. Quote: "APT achieves 91% average similarity to copyrighted originals", against 13.7% for random lyrics and 42.2% for semantic paraphrases. It works through the lyrics, not the title. — [arXiv 2507.17937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17937)
- **The Artist is Present (2511.17404): CONFIRMED.** Single author, Guilherme Coelho, 21 Nov 2025. The method uses metatag and descriptor combinations "without explicitly naming artists". It cites Newton-Rex (2024) "controlled prompt tests on Udio and Suno, using paraphrastic prompts that obliquely referenced specific songs to avoid name filters". It uses the direct Udio prompt "Philip Glass, einstein on the beach" (May 2024), with a figure showing Udio's "acceptance and name-to-descriptor expansion". The evidence is qualitative listening analysis on closed models. — [arXiv 2511.17404 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.17404)
- **Black-box membership inference (2605.29202): CONFIRMED.** Title "Auditing Training Data in Generative Music Models via Black-Box Membership Inference" (Liu, Yu, Cao, Meerza, Movva, Liu; 28 May 2026). Targets: "AudioLDM2, Stable Audio Open, and Mustango". Queries are attribute captions produced by Music Flamingo. Reports up to 98.6% accuracy. The example captions contain no titles. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29202)
- **Tracing Inputs, Verifying Outputs (2610.09637): PARTLY CONFIRMED.** Kim et al. (13 authors, including Juhan Nam and Jongpil Lee), 7 Oct 2026. **Not a survey.** It presents MixAudio, a generator conditioned on audio only ("condition the generation solely on audio without any text input"), and musicDNA, a version-identification model used as a memorization detector. Appendix C.1, Table 7, lists six earlier memorization audits: Jukebox (listening), MusicLM (token match, "Calibrated threshold"), MusicGen (token match, "Fixed 80% match"), Stable Audio 2 and Stable Audio Open (CLAP retrieval, "Manual threshold"), and YuE (ByteCover2). The paper does not use title prompts. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09637v1)
- **Anti-memorization guidance (2509.14934): CONFIRMED.** Title "Mitigating data replication in text-to-audio generative diffusion models through anti-memorization guidance" (Messina, Ronchini, Comanducci, Bestagini, Antonacci). Accepted at ICASSP 2026. Uses Stable Audio Open as the backbone because its architecture and training data are fully open. Does not use title prompts. — [arXiv 2509.14934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14934)
- **Batlle-Roca et al. (2407.14364): CONFIRMED.** Title "Towards Assessing Data Replication in Music Generation with Music Similarity Metrics on Raw Audio" (Batlle-Roca, Liao, Serra, Mitsufuji, Gómez). "Accepted at ISMIR 2024". Introduces the MiRA tool; "We evaluate the ability of five metrics to identify exact replication", which works when replication is above 10%. Uses synthetic samples and no prompts. — [arXiv 2407.14364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14364)
- **Generation or Replication (2310.10604): CONFIRMED (venue partly).** Bralios, Wichern, Germain, Pan, Khurana, Hori and Le Roux (MERL). Trains on AudioCaps (general audio, not music) and analyses memorization "as a function of training set size". Finds mel-spectrogram similarity more robust than learned embeddings. The arXiv page says only "submitted to ICASSP 2024"; I did not confirm the final venue. — [arXiv 2310.10604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10604)
- **Suno/Udio prompt analysis (2509.11824): PARTLY CONFIRMED.** Title "Data-Driven Analysis of Text-Conditioned AI-Generated Music: A Case Study with Suno and Udio" (Casini, Cros Vila, Dalmazzo, Kaila, Sturm; 15 Sep 2025). Covers user songs from May to Oct 2024 and mentions "peculiar attempts at steering models through the use of metatags". The arXiv page says "Submitted for review to TISMIR Digital Musicology special issue". The existing check's "TISMIR May 2026" publication is **UNVERIFIED**. — [arXiv 2509.11824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11824)
- **Melody or Machine (2512.00621): CONFIRMED.** Batra et al., 29 Nov 2025. §3.1.2, "Type A: Inspired by Existing Songs": "prompts were generated by reimagining existing song titles in a new musical style, with genres sampled from a curated list of 163 options derived from the FMA dataset." The benchmark's generators (Table 4) are Suno 1/2/3/3.5, Udio 1.5, Riffusion, DiffRhythm and YuE. Titles are used only to generate data; nothing is measured from them. — [arXiv 2512.00621 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.00621)
- **ACE-Step 1.5 tech report (2602.00744): CONFIRMED.** It describes "a Qwen-based Language Model" producing CoT metadata (BPM, key, duration, structure) in YAML: "by outputting metadata in a standardized YAML format". `lm_metadata` comes from the repo docs, not the report (see Q2). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00744v1); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00744)

### Inferences
- None of the verified papers uses a title-only prompt as an experimental variable on an open model. Melody or Machine uses title prompts only to build a dataset, and Coelho's "work title + artist" prompt is a single anecdote on Udio.
- Ghost's statement of a "1.8M-song" corpus looks borrowed from ACE-Step **v1**. The v1 paper says "approximately 1.8 million unique musical pieces (roughly 100,000 hours)" ([arXiv 2506.00045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00045)), while the v1.5 report gives a "27-million-sample corpus". Neither the 1.8M figure nor the v1.5 corpus size should be cited as established for v1.5.

## Q2. ACE-Step 1.5 technical facts needed to run Idea B

### Takeaway
The planner is a Qwen3-based language model (0.6B, 1.7B or 4B). The Python API exposes its output (`extra_outputs["lm_metadata"]`, `cot_*` fields) and lets you fix BPM, key and caption yourself. **One catch for the clamp design:** with `thinking=True` the planner writes not only metadata but also 5 Hz *audio semantic codes* that condition the diffusion model. Clamping BPM and key therefore does not cut every channel from the planner to the audio. Also, even with the planner off, captions are encoded by Qwen3-0.6B, which has its own world knowledge. The training data is described only as a marketing claim ("legally compliant" licensed, royalty-free and synthetic music), with no list of sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Planner:** the LM checkpoints acestep-5Hz-lm-0.6B, 1.7B and 4B are "pretrained from Qwen3-0.6B / Qwen3-1.7B / Qwen3-4B". The model card describes the LM as an "omni-capable planner" that produces song blueprints. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/Ace-Step1.5); [GitHub README](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)
- **Diffusion-model conditioning:** "Qwen3-0.6B caption embeddings are concatenated with dedicated timbre and lyric encoders" through cross-attention. — [tech report](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00744v1). Ghost adds that the lyric encoder is "an 8-layer transformer and Qwen3 token embeddings". — [arXiv 2609.39552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39552)
- **The planner emits audio codes, not just metadata:** `thinking` is documented as "Enable 5Hz Language Model 'Chain-of-Thought' reasoning for semantic/music metadata and codes". The Tutorial says the LM "Generates **semantic codes**" that feed the DiT. The API also accepts `audio_codes` ("Pre-extracted 5Hz audio semantic codes as a string. Advanced use only."). An analysis function can "Analyze audio semantic codes and extract metadata (caption, lyrics, BPM, key, etc.)". — [INFERENCE.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/INFERENCE.md); [Tutorial.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/Tutorial.md)
- **Exposed metadata:** `GenerationParams` has `bpm` (Optional[int], 30–300, "None enables auto-detection via LM"), `keyscale` ("Empty string enables auto-detection"), `timesignature`, `duration`, `caption` (≤512 characters), `thinking` (default True), `use_cot_metas`, `use_cot_caption`, `use_cot_language` (all default True), `use_cot_lyrics` (reserved), and `use_constrained_decoding` (default True; "FSM-based constrained decoding", i.e. the output is forced into a fixed format). It also has LM-filled fields `cot_bpm`, `cot_keyscale`, `cot_timesignature`, `cot_duration`, `cot_vocal_language`, `cot_caption` and `cot_lyrics`. Results carry `extra_outputs` with "latents, masks, lm_metadata, time_costs", and the docs give the example `result.extra_outputs["lm_metadata"]['bpm']` / `['keyscale']`. — [INFERENCE.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/INFERENCE.md)
- **Clamping and overrides:** `format_sample(..., user_metadata={"bpm": 95})` is described as "Optional: constrain specific values (bpm, duration, keyscale, timesignature, language)". The docs also show the pattern `use_cot_metas=False  # Already formatted, skip metas CoT` with `thinking=True` and explicit bpm/keyscale. The Tutorial says parameters "are **guidance** rather than **precise commands**" and that the planning step can be skipped entirely "by not using `thinking` mode". The LM is skipped automatically for cover, repaint and extract tasks. — [INFERENCE.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/INFERENCE.md); [Tutorial.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/Tutorial.md)
- **Training data, as the model card claims it:** "trained on a massive, legally compliant dataset consisting of: Licensed Data: Professionally licensed music tracks. Royalty-Free / No-Copyright Data: A vast collection of public domain and royalty-free music. Synthetic Data: High-quality audio generated via advanced MIDI-to-Audio conversion." — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/Ace-Step1.5)
- **Training data, as the tech report describes it:** a "Golden Set of 5 million audio samples" annotated by Gemini 2.5 Pro, then "our entire 27-million-sample corpus" annotated by in-house captioners fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-Omni. No sources are named, and memorization, copyright and title filtering are not discussed. The GitHub README does not describe training data. — [tech report PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00744); [README](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)
- **Conflicting statements:** Ghost calls the corpus "undocumented" and reports cover-retrieval of popular pre-2024 songs above chance (Q0). That is hard to square with a corpus of only licensed, royalty-free and MIDI-rendered music, unless the "licensed" portion includes commercial catalogue. — [arXiv 2609.39552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.39552) vs [HF card](https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/Ace-Step1.5). A community thread asks "do we now have an ethically trained Suno at home?" (I did not open it). — [VI-Control](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/ace-step-1-5-do-we-now-have-an-ethically-trained-suno-at-home.170009/)
- **Licence:** "This project is licensed under MIT", and the Hugging Face card says `license: mit`. The card says users "can strictly use the generated music for **commercial purposes**". The README disclaimer acknowledges "unintentional copyright infringement due to stylistic similarity" and asks users to "verify the originality of generated works". — [README](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/Ace-Step1.5)
- **GPU memory and speed:** "under 2 seconds per full song on an A100 and under 10 seconds on an RTX 3090"; "less than 4GB of VRAM"; "0.5s to 10s on A100 depending on think mode & diffusion steps". The Tutorial's LM guidance: "VRAM < 8GB → No LM or 0.6B", "8–16GB → 1.7B (default)", "> 16GB → 1.7B or 4B". The XL diffusion model needs "~9GB VRAM for weights", with a minimum of 12 GB using offload plus quantization and 20 GB+ recommended. — [README](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5); [Tutorial.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/Tutorial.md)
- **Prompting examples:** the Tutorial itself uses artist references such as "reminiscent of Bon Iver". It says nothing about song names and lists no title or artist filter. — [Tutorial.md](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5/blob/main/docs/en/Tutorial.md)

### Inferences
- A clean decomposition needs at least four arms per title, not two: (A) `thinking=False`, title in the caption only (diffusion model plus the Qwen3-0.6B caption encoder); (B) `thinking=True`, free planner (log `lm_metadata`, `cot_caption`, `audio_codes`); (C) `thinking=True` with `use_cot_metas=False` and BPM/key fixed to neutral or wrong-title values (the metadata clamp); (D) planner audio codes or caption from the wrong-title run swapped in (the code/caption clamp). Arm C alone cannot separate "planner metadata" from "planner audio codes".
- No arm of ACE-Step is free of a language model's world knowledge, because the caption encoder is Qwen3. The cleanest "knows the name but never heard the song" contrast is the MusicGen/T5-base arm (Q3), not ACE-Step with the planner switched off.
- Combined with the RTX 3090 timing, 4 arms × 3 title conditions (true, wrong, none) × a few seeds × hundreds of songs is feasible in GPU-hours. The cost is in the scoring: tempo, key and CLEWS cover retrieval.
- The model card's licensing claim alongside Ghost's above-chance cover retrieval is itself something Idea B could test (title-triggered resemblance to commercial originals). Phrase it as an observation, not an accusation.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm from the docs whether a user-supplied `bpm` or `keyscale` with `use_cot_metas=True` is written into the planner's prompt, forced through constrained decoding, or overwritten. Read `acestep/inference.py` / `llm_handler` before relying on it.
- I could not confirm whether the audio codes are generated *after* and *conditioned on* the YAML metadata. The Tutorial implies metadata and then codes, but I did not check the code.
- No separate weights licence file was found beyond the card's `license: mit`.

## Q3. Training data and text encoders of MusicGen and Stable Audio Open ("knowing without hearing")

### Takeaway
MusicGen was trained on 20K hours of licensed stock and internal music with vocals removed, and uses a T5 text encoder. Stable Audio Open was trained on Freesound plus 970 h of FMA (Free Music Archive) music screened against a copyright database, uses T5-base, and *did* see FMA title and artist strings in its training prompts. Both make good "has probably never heard the hit" controls. Their text encoders are small, though, so a null title effect is a likely outcome and should be planned for.

### Cited Findings
- MusicGen: "We use 20K hours of licensed music to train MUSICGEN. Specifically, we rely on an internal dataset of 10K high-quality music tracks, and on the ShutterStock and Pond5 music data collections with respectively 25K and 365K instrument-only [tracks]". "For text conditioning, we use the T5 [Raffel et al., 2020] text encoder". The paper's ablation compares T5 (t5-base), Flan-T5 (flan-t5-base) and CLAP. On ethics: "all the data we trained on was covered by legal agreements with the right holders". — [Copet et al., arXiv 2306.05284 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05284v3)
- MusicGen model card: trained on "the Meta Music Initiative Sound Collection, Shutterstock music collection and the Pond5 music collection"; "Vocals have been removed from the data source using corresponding tags, and then using a state-of-the-art music source separation method"; "The model is not able to generate realistic vocals"; "model weights are released under CC-BY-NC 4.0". — [HF musicgen-small card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/musicgen-small)
- Stable Audio Open: "486,492 recordings (7,300 h)", of which "472,618 (6,330 h) are from Freesound and 13,874 (970 h) from FMA". Freesound music was flagged with PANNs and checked by "a trusted content detection company". For FMA, "We did a metadata search against a large database of copyrighted music". "FMA music examples include metadata like year, genres, album, title, and artist", which were concatenated into prompts (example: "year: 2021, artist: dadabots, album: can't play instruments, title: pizza hangover"). "Text conditioning is provided by the pretrained T5-base encoder." — [Evans et al., arXiv 2407.14358](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14358)

### Inferences
- In Stable Audio Open, "title:" and "artist:" are trained prompt fields tied to FMA audio. Prompting "title: Billie Jean, artist: Michael Jackson" therefore uses a format the model knows, but the content (famous Billboard songs) should be absent after copyright screening. This makes it a strong "knows the field, not the song" control.
- MusicGen was trained without vocals and on stock music, so any song-specific tempo or key shift for a famous title would have to come from T5-base's text knowledge. T5-base (about 220M parameters) probably knows little about specific songs' tempo or key. Expect near-null effects, which is useful as a negative control but weak as a headline.
- Neither paper explicitly says famous commercial songs are absent. "Plausibly absent" remains an inference, as the existing check correctly says.

### Gaps
- The Stable Audio Open weights licence could not be fetched (the Hugging Face card is gated, and the raw README returned nothing).
- I found no study that probes T5, CLAP or Qwen3 text encoders for song-level facts (tempo, key, era) as used inside music generators. A web search on "text encoder world knowledge text-to-music" returned only general claims (for example, the MIDI-LLM motivation that LLMs "encode broad world knowledge") ([arXiv 2511.03942](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03942v1)), and no measurements.

## Q4. Any study, blog, notebook, issue or forum thread, and any 2026 venue paper, on title-conditioned memorization or "entity priors" in music generation

### Takeaway
Nothing found. The closest items are Ghost in the Encoder (ISMIR 2026; lyrics, with the planner on and off), Beyond Prompted Agreement (instruction following on ACE-Step 1.5), Melody or Machine (title prompts used only to build data), and Coelho's single Udio anecdote. ### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Taking the competition risk at face value: Ghost's own "future work" sentence makes the planner-as-recognition-trigger question an announced next step for that group. The existing check's 20–30% scoop estimate for B before May 2027 looks, if anything, low for the *planner* component. It is still reasonable for a *title-only, attribute-scored* paper.
