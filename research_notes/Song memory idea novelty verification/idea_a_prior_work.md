# Idea A ("Frankenstein songs"): adversarial prior-work verification (as of 2026-10-22)

Idea A: give audio-language models (LALMs) edited versions of famous songs (stem-swapped, stem-removed, covers, karaoke) with the true title, a wrong title or no title. Then test whether the model reports what the edited audio contains or what it remembers about the song. The existing novelty check (`D:\WMM\Adversarial Novelty Check_ Frankenstein Songs and Generators Remember Songs by Title.md`) scores it 4/5.

## Q1. Are the novelty document's claims about named prior work correct?

### Takeaway
Most claims hold up against the primary sources: 8 CONFIRMED, 4 PARTLY, 0 WRONG. The partial ones have three problems: (a) the MUB quote "memorized recording associations, and external retrieval" could not be found verbatim, (b) MUB has run no models at all, and (c) the Music Flamingo remark comes from a qualitative user study on only 8 songs. One factual detail is worth knowing: the "answer as if listening" instruction is in LLark's and Audio Flamingo 2's prompts word for word, and Audio Flamingo 2 also says "Do not mention or ask about the track title, artist, or album."

### Cited Findings
- **POPEv2 (arXiv 2508.04567): CONFIRMED.** Li, Zhou, Zhao, Fang, Wen, submitted 6 Aug 2025. The HTML says "We randomly sample 500 images from the MSCOCO 2017 training set", made by "masking the object with a black patch" (supplement: "replaced by a black mask using the bounding box provided in the MSCOCO annotations"). The main table gives LLaVA-1.5-7B a true-negative rate (TNR, the share of correct "no" answers on the masked images) of 49.00, and the text says "most models do not even exceed 70%". The bias is located in the LM head (the output layer): "the LM head, which is responsible for producing output text, fails to fully leverage" the object signals. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04567); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04567)
- **"Beyond Text Following" / GACL (arXiv 2606.05161): CONFIRMED.** Gao, Zhang, Wang, … Daling Wang; v1 3 Jun 2026; filed under cs.SD (sound). Quote: "64.1% of conflict samples show a sign flip". This covers five audio-language models and four conflict tasks. The same-audio branch prefers the audio-supported answer while the joint branch prefers the text-supported one. GACL = "Gated Audio Counterfactual Logit Correction", a fix applied at decoding time with no retraining. The abstract does not mention music, song identity or parametric memory. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05161)
- **ALME / "When Audio-LLMs Don't Listen" (arXiv 2602.11488): CONFIRMED.** Sole author Jayadev Billa (unaffiliated). Text Dominance Ratio (TDR, how often the model follows the text over the audio): 16.6% for Gemini 2.0 Flash and 23.2% for GPT-4o; cascade baselines 1.6% and 0.9%. The clustered bootstrap table gives slightly different values, 16.5% and 23.1%. The paper scopes out memory explicitly: "rather than contextual evidence versus parametric memory, we study conflict between two external sources presented at inference time, one spoken and one textual." Speech only (Common Voice); the Limitations section says it does not cover "non-speech audio". — [arXiv html v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11488v3)
- **"Who Wins the Conflict?" (arXiv 2606.18924): CONFIRMED.** Cho, Yoo, Jang, Changick Kim, Joon Son Chung; affiliation "Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology"; v1 17 Jun 2026, v2 4 Sep 2026; EMNLP 2026 Main. Quote: "we leverage back-patching, a training-free intervention that routes late-layer audio activations back into earlier layers." It is built on ALME, with four speech conflict tasks (adjective, negation, number and time swaps). No music and no song memory. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18924); [html v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18924v2)
- **MUB identity-masked benchmark (arXiv 2610.22989): PARTLY.** Kun Liu and Lyra Tao; v1 16 Oct 2026; 500 recordings. Quote: "Original titles, artists, and platform identifiers are excluded from evaluation inputs." Section 4.1 says "an instruction not to use prior knowledge cannot remove memorized recording information from model parameters". The abstract says evaluation "requires separating audio evidence from answers recoverable through metadata or external tools". The document's exact quote ("memorized recording associations, and external retrieval") was **not found**. The novelty document also leaves out that MUB **reports no model runs at all**. It lists audio-present vs audio-absent comparisons and audio-removal controls as future work, and it has no identity-present arm, covers or edited audio. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22989); [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22989)
- **Beyond Binary Instrument QA (arXiv 2606.31338): CONFIRMED.** Yujun Lee, Joonhyeok Shin, Hyoeun Kim, Kyuhong Shim; 30 Jun 2026. Comments field: "Workshop on Machine Learning for Audio, ICML 2026" (the "ML4Audio" label is the document's shorthand). Built from OpenMIC, with examples where genre gives fewer hints, confusable instruments, option-position bias and timing bias. The abstract does not mention song identity or stem editing. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31338)
- **"Do Audio-Visual LLMs Really See and Hear?" (arXiv 2604.02605): CONFIRMED.** Selvakumar, Jayakumar, Sakshi, **Sreyan Ghosh**, Ruohan Gao, Dinesh Manocha; 3 Apr 2026; CVPR Findings. Quote: "This yields 250 factual samples (original audio-video pairs) and 250 counterfactual samples (mismatched pairs)". Built from AudioCaps by swapping in a dissimilar audio track with FFmpeg (pairs chosen by Hungarian matching on caption similarity). The study centres on non-speech sound events, not music. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02605)
- **ROHE (NAACL 2025): CONFIRMED.** He, Sun, Ren, Wang et al., "Evaluating and Mitigating Object Hallucination in Large Vision-Language Models: Can They Still See Removed Objects?" PDF text: "we introduce ROHE (Removed Object Hallucination Evaluation benchmark)… ROHE sequentially removes unique objects using LaMa". The abstract says LVLMs "often still claim to see the removed objects". It has 5,504 hand-checked images. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.349/); [PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.349.pdf)
- **Music Flamingo remark (arXiv 2511.10289): CONFIRMED, but weaker evidence than the document suggests.** It appears in Appendix E, Table 4 (user study), GPT-4o-Audio column: "Relies on recognition of famous songs; may pull from text knowledge instead of audio" and "Gives more detail when recognizing famous songs; otherwise shallow". The introduction adds that frontier models "sometimes rely on text-derived knowledge rather than genuine auditory analysis." The basis is a qualitative user study on **8 songs** (4 English, 4 Brazilian Portuguese; "half of them are by extremely popular artists", e.g. ABBA "Money Money Money", Michael Sembello "Maniac"). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10289v1); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10289)
- **LLark prompts (arXiv 2310.07160): CONFIRMED (word for word).** From the PDF text: "DO NOT disclose that metadata about the song is provided to you. Always answer as if you are an expert who is listening to the audio." and "respond as if you are hearing the song and reporting facts about what you hear." Appendix F.1.3 says the generator asked about "metadata fields which we provided but instructed it not to ask about (e.g. artist, song title)". So title and artist were in the metadata given to the generator. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.07160)
- **Audio Flamingo 2 prompts (arXiv 2503.03983): CONFIRMED.** It reuses LLark-style prompts (Figs. 35–37; "borrowed from Gardner et al. (2023)"). The text includes "DO NOT disclose that metadata about the song is provided to you. Always answer as if you are an expert who is listening to the audio." and "Do not mention or ask about the track title, artist, or album." — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03983)
- **Music Flamingo prompts: PARTLY (the wording differs from LLark's).** Appendix H, MF-Think prompt: "The thinking trace should therefore be written as if listening to the track directly, not using metadata. Metadata is provided only to help you avoid mistakes". I could not confirm from the text that its metadata includes the song title. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10289)
- **STEMMA (arXiv 2610.11884): CONFIRMED that it has no removed or swapped stems.** See Q3. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11884)
- **MuseDiag (arXiv 2609.20195), checked because the document relied on a subagent's reading: CONFIRMED.** Quote: "We explicitly exclude factual memory hallucination (e.g., fabricating composers or release years)… which reflects failures of external knowledge rather than music perception." It uses MusicCaps (440 clips) and IRMAS, with no edited audio. Its affiliations are CAS-IIE/UCAS, Central Conservatory of Music and UESTC. The HTML header shows a date of "24 Jul 2026", which looks odd next to a 2609 ID; check this before citing. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20195)

## Q2. Has Idea A already been done, fully or partly? (closing the document's coverage gaps)

### Takeaway
I found **no paper, preprint, workshop paper or GitHub repository** that gives LALMs edited audio of famous songs (stem-swapped, stem-removed, cover, karaoke or remix) and tests memory against hearing, with or without a title cue. However, the document **missed the closest design precedent**. "Vision Language Models are Biased" (VLMBias, arXiv 2505.23941) does exactly Idea A's logic in vision. It uses counterfactual edits of *famous, recognizable* subjects. Models score 100% on the originals and 17.05% on the edits, and **adding the subject's name as text lowers accuracy by a further 2–6 points**. That is the analogue of Idea A's "true title" arm. A reviewer will call A "VLMBias for music" more readily than "POPEv2 for music". My adversarial score: **3.5/5**. The music version is new, but the design and the main claim exist in vision.

### Cited Findings
- **VLMBias (arXiv 2505.23941)**: Vo, K.-N. Nguyen, Taesiri, Dang, Anh Totti Nguyen, Daeyoung Kim; v1 29 May 2025, v4 19 Apr 2026. Quote: models are "unable to recognize the 4th stripe has been added to a 3-stripe Adidas logo". "The mean accuracy of the five VLMs across the seven tasks is 17.05%", and on unmodified images "All five VLMs score 100% accuracy". With the subject name added to the image, "VLM counting accuracy further drops by −2 to −6 points". Accuracy does **not** follow the authors' popularity ordering (animals 2.12%, chess pieces 26.25%, optical illusions 50.87%). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23941); [html](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23941). A search result lists it as an ICLR 2026 poster ([iclr.cc listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10010794), seen only as a search result, not opened); the arXiv page itself lists no venue.
- Searches for LALMs on covers, karaoke, instrumental versions or remixes of famous songs returned only cover-*identification* (retrieval) and cover-*generation* work. Examples are cover-ID robustness, where "drum-only versions, instrumental versions and medleys" are hardest for retrieval models ([arXiv 2501.01333](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01333)), and the Kara1k karaoke dataset ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322201134_Kara1k_A_Karaoke_Dataset_for_Cover_Song_Identification_and_Singing_Voice_Analysis)). No LALM evaluation turned up.
- Searches for "song identification by LALMs" turned up general music QA benchmarks, not recognition studies. The nearest is MusICA-MetaBench ("Music I Care About", [arXiv 2607.06015](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06015v1)). It builds questions from MusicXML scores, with no audio edits and no famous songs. It mentions leakage only as a reason for generating questions on demand: "benchmark data may leak into future training corpora".
- HalluAudio (ACL 2026, [arXiv 2604.19300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19300)) uses "adversarial prompts and mixed-audio conditions" across speech, sound and music. The abstract mentions no stem removal or swap, famous songs or memorization (full text not checked).
- A GitHub repository, "Diagnosing Text Bias in Audio-Language Models with a Contamination-Free Benchmark" ([yunaLee21](https://github.com/yunaLee21/audio-text-bias-diagnosis)), names concept *familiarity* as a confound ("a dog bark carries a strong pretraining prior"). It avoids familiarity by inventing creatures and speaking about them with text-to-speech. It is speech only; a non-speech extension is planned and music is not mentioned. Its authors and paper ID are not shown.
- The MusicListenBench leaderboard ([GitHub](https://github.com/AMAAI-Lab/MusicListenBench), AMAAI Lab) asks "Can audio LLMs hear the difference yet?" (4 aspects, "melody…harmony", 500 items each). Its Hugging Face dataset page says "The dataset is currently empty" ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/amaai-lab/MusicListenBench)). I could not see how items are built or whether it uses paired or edited audio. **Watch this one.**

  - NeurIPS 2026 workshops: 102 accepted, but no accepted-paper lists for music workshops were found ([NeurIPS blog](https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/08/10/announcing-the-neurips-2026-workshops/)).
  - ICML 2026 audio workshop page: [icml.cc](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/workshop/54087). The one music-and-LALM paper I found there is Beyond Binary Instrument QA.

- Other near-misses (all checked; none have famous-song edits): the ALME / "Who Wins" / GACL text-dominance line (speech, typed conflicts); MCR-Bench "When Audio and Text Disagree" ([arXiv 2508.15407](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15407)); "Membership Inference Attacks against LALMs" ([arXiv 2603.28378](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.28378), which includes NSynth music synthesis, not famous recordings); "Knowing When Not to Answer" ([arXiv 2609.04362](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04362), abstention in music LALMs).

### Inferences
- **The closest 5 works for Idea A (what each does, what it lacks):**
  1. **VLMBias (2505.23941).** Famous-subject counterfactual edits, recognition sanity check, and a name-cue arm that makes things worse. Lacks audio and music, removal or substitution of a component inside a mixture, a no-cue arm in which the model recognizes the subject from the signal alone, and a popularity dose-response (popularity did not predict accuracy).
  2. **Music Flamingo Appendix E (2511.10289).** Observes that GPT-4o-Audio and Gemini lean on famous-song recognition and hallucinate drums and synths. Lacks a controlled design, edited audio and a title manipulation; n=8 and qualitative.
  3. **POPEv2 (2508.04567) / ROHE (NAACL 2025).** Remove an object from images (POPEv2 uses training images) and ask whether it is there; they find hallucination and attribute it to training bias. Lack audio, entity identity and a title cue (removal is generic, not famous-specific).
  4. **The ALME (2602.11488) → "Who Wins the Conflict?" (2606.18924) → GACL (2606.05161) cluster.** Audio vs typed-text conflict in LALMs, with mechanism and decoding fixes. Lacks music and memory triggered by recognition; ALME explicitly scopes out "contextual evidence versus parametric memory".
  5. **MUB (2610.22989).** Names the confound (memorized recording information) and removes identity from music evaluation. Lacks model results, an identity-present arm and any edited audio.
  (Honourable mention: "Do AV-LLMs Really See and Hear?" (2604.02605). It swaps real signals across modalities, 250 pairs, but uses no music and no identity.)
- The novelty case for A now rests on: (i) music and audio as the modality; (ii) **edits inside the mix** (stem substitution, not just removal), with ground truth known by construction from multitrack stems; (iii) separating **recognition from the signal alone** (no title) from a **typed title** cue, which VLMBias does not cleanly separate because its name cue is text pasted into the image; (iv) karaoke and cover versions as realistic, non-synthetic counterfactuals; (v) a recognition-gated dose-response. VLMBias's null popularity result makes (v) a real open question rather than an obvious one.
- The training-prompt evidence (LLark and AF2 telling the generator to answer "as if you are an expert who is listening to the audio", AF2 with "Do not mention… track title, artist") gives Idea A a mechanism to test. That is a strength VLMBias lacks.

## Q3. Does any work use MUSDB18/MoisesDB/MedleyDB stems to build counterfactual mixes for LALM evaluation?

### Takeaway
No. STEMMA, the only stem-aware LALM benchmark found, has **no condition** that removes, swaps or remixes stems. Its "hard negatives" are near-misses drawn from its catalog, and its coreference training *concatenates* separate songs one after another rather than mixing them.

### Cited Findings
- **STEMMA** (arXiv 2610.11884, 8 Oct 2026; Sohn, W. Kim, K. Kim, Kum, T. Kim, Moon, Charoenchob, Weerapang, Jongpil Lee, Juhan Nam; affiliations "Graduate School of Culture Technology, KAIST" and "Neutune"):
  - "Every excerpt corresponds to one section and contains either the full section mixture or one separated role."
  - Hard negatives: "a single catalog query retrieves both matching examples and near-misses that violate it."
  - Coreference: "Two to four full songs from distinct tracks are concatenated under separate labels."
  - Data: an in-house "licensing-cleared catalog" (15,856 songs), JamendoMaxCaps and an FMA subset. MUSDB18, MedleyDB and MoisesDB are evaluation sources; stems are either authored or separated with SCNet (a separation model).
  - Prompts use neutral letters, not titles. There is no discussion of memorization. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11884); [GitHub](https://github.com/hoyso48/STEMMA)
- A search for removed-instrument ("minus-one") evaluation of LALMs found only separation work: SAM Audio's removal evaluation ([arXiv 2512.18099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18099)) and the SDX 2023 human listening tests on segments with one instrument extracted or removed ([arXiv 2308.06979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.06979)). No LALM benchmark. — [search result sources as listed]

### Inferences
- Using multitrack stems to build counterfactual mixes for LALM evaluation is still unclaimed. MUSDB18-HQ and MoisesDB songs are mostly little-known, though, so they serve the "unrecognized" control arm, not the famous-song arm. This matches the project's existing Task 2 removal measurements in `data/features/ablation/`.

## Q4. Which groups are most likely to publish this next?

### Takeaway
Based on recent papers (my inference, not sourced predictions), the most likely groups are, in order: (1) UMD GAMMA (Manocha) together with NVIDIA, through Sreyan Ghosh; (2) KAIST's Chung lab; (3) the VLMBias authors (Auburn and KAIST); (4) KAIST's Nam lab with Neutune; (5) the MUB authors.

### Cited Findings
- Sreyan Ghosh is a co-author of both the cross-modal real-signal-swap counterfactual paper ([2604.02605](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02605), Manocha group) and Music Flamingo, which made the famous-song remark ([2511.10289](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10289v1)).
- The KAIST Chung lab has already extended ALME with mechanistic analysis and back-patching (EMNLP 2026) ([2606.18924](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18924v2)).
- The VLMBias authors (Anh Totti Nguyen, Daeyoung Kim) own the famous-subject counterfactual design ([2505.23941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23941)).
- The KAIST Nam lab and Neutune have stem infrastructure and a licensed catalog ([STEMMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11884)).
- MUB lists audio-removal controls as future work ([2610.22989](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22989)).
- The yunaLee21 repository plans non-speech fictitious sounds and names familiarity as a confound ([GitHub](https://github.com/yunaLee21/audio-text-bias-diagnosis)).
- Kyuhong Shim's group already reduces genre priors in instrument QA ([2606.31338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31338)).

### Inferences
- Ghosh/Manocha/NVIDIA is the single most dangerous group. They hold both halves, the observation (Music Flamingo) and the counterfactual-swap method (the audio-visual paper), plus the training-data pipeline that causes the effect.
- A port of VLMBias to audio ("audio LMs are biased") by its authors is plausible and would most likely use environmental sounds or famous jingles rather than stems. Even so, it would weaken A's claim to the concept.
- Recommendation for positioning: cite VLMBias as the main design precedent, and stress what VLMBias lacks: (a) a no-cue arm that tests recognition from the signal alone, (b) substitution inside a mixture, and (c) a dose-response, given that VLMBias found no popularity trend. Put the stimulus set online with a timestamp early.
